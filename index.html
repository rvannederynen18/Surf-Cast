Year over year at a NOAA station

Tap the chart to jump the map to that date.

Each colored band is where that fish is expected on the chosen date, and the dot is the center of the run. The colored line along the beach is the predicted water temperature for that date. It starts from typical seasonal temperatures for each latitude and is corrected with today's measured water temperature from NOAA stations and buoys (the small round markers on the map show the measured readings), with the correction fading over about six weeks. The moon box in the corner shows the moon and tide strength for the chosen date. Solid bands are where the predicted water is also inside that fish's range, and dashed gray is where the calendar says it should be but the water is too warm or too cold. Warmer-than-normal water holds runs farther north, and colder water pushes them south. Bull redfish, black drum, and whiting are mostly local movers, so they are not drawn as routes.

Scores come from tide, wind, surf, water temperature, season, light, and moon rules. They are estimates, not guarantees.
